## Baseline: why this market needs helical piers

- **Groundwater subsidence**: Houston-Galveston region subsidence is driven by
  compaction of clay/silt aquifer sediments from groundwater withdrawal. Satellite data
  showed up to ~9 cm of subsidence north/northwest/west of Houston 2016–2020 (~2 cm/yr)
  (SRC-031). Spring Branch and areas north of Houston historically affected; regulation
  has slowed but not eliminated movement (SRC-030, SRC-032).
- **Expansive clay**: Texas expansive clays ("black gumbo") can swell up to ~12% by
  volume and exert pressures over 10,000 psf; they account for roughly 65% of Texas
  foundation problems. Shallow slab-on-grade homes feel every shrink/swell cycle
  (SRC-033 for design guidance).
- **Regulatory driver**: Harris-Galveston Subsidence District (est. 1975) permits
  groundwater wells and pushes conversion to surface water. Spring/Klein water entities
  are actively converting to surface water under HGSD rules (Community Impact, 2025-02).
  HGSD runs HoustonNet, ~250 permanent GNSS stations monitoring subsidence, and has a
  Spring Creek subsidence/flood-risk study directly relevant to our service area
  (SRC-030, SRC-034).
