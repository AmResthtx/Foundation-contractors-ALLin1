# Company Content & Publishing Policies

## Purpose
We position our company as a trusted source of quality information for the helical pier industry. These policies guide how we research, source, publish, and improve content so that it educates homeowners, engineers, contractors, and the local community while protecting the company's traceability needs.

## Policy 1 — Positioning
- Our content must be accurate, professional, and focused on educating the public and trade professionals about helical piers, their applications, benefits, and limitations.
- Content should highlight local projects and community impact where relevant (for example, local sinkhole or subsidence events) to demonstrate real-world value.

## Policy 2 — Sourcing & Attribution
- Cite external sources with links when they inform or support our content. Public credit must be given to the original author or organization when material from them is used.
- Where we need to track sources internally, use company-only traceability (internal tracking links, tags, or a private reference ID) while keeping the public-facing citation normal and visible.
- Prioritize authoritative sources (peer-reviewed studies, engineering standards, government reports, recognized trade organizations, or manufacturer technical documents).

## Policy 3 — Market & Events Monitoring
- Monitor only the steel prices and specific alloys that directly affect our procurement and cost structure. Maintain a short list of alloy codes (exact material spec) used in our projects.
- Research and record exact alloy codes and relevant technical data (e.g., ASTM/AISI/EN codes) for the steel products we use.
- Actively watch local events and incidents that make helical piers relevant (for example, sinkholes, notable foundation failures, or infrastructure distress). Use such events as timely educational opportunities to explain why helical piers can be an appropriate solution, emphasizing community reinvestment ("We spend our money here at home").

## Policy 4 — Content Success & Continuous Improvement
- Success metrics are flexible. Viral reach is a valid success outcome if the content is relevant to some part of our job process and educates the audience.
- Capture a range of perspectives for content creation: customer testimonials, crew/installer perspectives, engineering commentary, and third-party feedback. Document both positive experiences and criticism; use feedback to improve process and content.
- Self-improvement is graded on adherence and demonstrated learning; it should be applied flexibly rather than as a strict pass/fail.

## Use of Local Projects in Messaging
- When using a local project to illustrate our work (e.g., Bammel North Houston, Veteran Memorial area), ensure we have permission to publish project details and any identifiable photos/quotes.
- Emphasize community benefit: local hiring, local procurement, and reinvestment in the area.

## Legal & Ethical
- Do not publish private customer data or identifiable health/safety information without explicit consent.
- Verify technical claims with an engineer when statements concern structural performance, safety, or code compliance.
